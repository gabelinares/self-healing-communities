A different way of working

SHCF brings evidence, community knowledge, learning, and financing together in a continuing cycle.

Pick a step

Five steps wind outward from the core. Choose any one and the path lights from the centre out to it — what it means appears here.

Risk
Stewardship

  1. Find leverage

    See connected risk and healing trajectories across systems, the life course, and generations. Use rigorous evidence and community knowledge to identify where change may have the greatest reach.

  2. Build effective portfolios

    Bring together community knowledge, lived experience, research, practice, public-system authority, and resources to build locally tailored portfolios that change underlying risk trajectories and strengthen protective capacity. Investments work together across the places where risk develops, building on the relationships, assets, and capacities already present.

  3. Learn continuously across investments

    Create shared feedback so communities and partners can learn together whether risk is changing, which benefits cascade across systems, where public costs change, and what should adapt, be sustained, or scale.

  4. Finance differently

    Finance portfolios that reduce risk earlier, strengthen community capacity, support learning, and create benefits across systems. Track the human, economic, and fiscal value created — and align incentives so public systems are rewarded for reducing risk earlier, not only responding to its consequences.

  5. Make improvement durable

    Connect learning, public financing, provider sustainability, and reinvestment so successful change does not disappear when a grant ends. Create mechanisms to reinvest financial gains from improved outcomes into risk reduction and community capacity.

What success returns ↓

SHCF is not another answer to the question, “What should we fund upstream?” It is infrastructure for continually answering harder questions together:

What is driving risk? Where can its course be changed? How do we know? What is strengthening the protective capacities that support health, safety, and prosperity? What should be adapted, sustained, or scaled? And how do we align leadership, resources, and financial incentives to act on what we learn?

What success returns

Success creates value in more than one way.

01

Human return

Better health, safety, learning, stability, belonging, relationships, and wellbeing.

02

Economic return

Greater participation in work, earnings, productivity, family economic stability, and community economic vitality.

03

Fiscal return

Reduced or more stable demand for costly public services and, where demonstrated, measurable improvements in public costs or revenues.

The result is a different way of working: communities and public systems move from repeatedly paying for the consequences of risk toward continually learning how to reduce it — and building the capacity to keep doing so.

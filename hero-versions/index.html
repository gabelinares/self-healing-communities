The Self-Healing Communities Fund is building a new model that helps communities and public systems to align people, funding, and practices around the common causes of lifelong wellbeing.

Self-Healing Communities Fund
It begins with seeing differently.
“I used to think we had lots of separate problems. Then I realized we were seeing the same problem from different departments, the same people at different moments in their lives.
City Commissioner

We keep treating and financing connected problems as separate ones. Homelessness, behavioral health, chronic disease, addiction, family instability, violence, and problems at school and work are shaped by common causes.

Page one
Risk can build over time.

The Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs) discoveries show how risk can accumulate across childhood, potentially affecting health, learning, family stability, work, and involvement with public systems across a lifetime.

“Every experience teaches the body and brain what to expect next. When families experience high levels of stress over and over, risk builds over time and can affect the life course and the next generation.
Dr. Robert Anda
Page two
ACE-related risk has outgrown the systems built to manage it.
27%increase in average ACE score among adults 25–39, 2009–2010 to 2023–2024
1 in 4adults that age now report 4+ ACEs, up from about 1 in 6 a decade earlier

This creates a structural mismatch. The public systems responding to the consequences of adversity were largely built in an earlier, lower-prevalence era — before we understood how ACE-related risk can accumulate across the life course, cross systems, and carry forward to the next generation.

“The bills show up in one place, the savings show up somewhere else, and they don't happen until years later. Addressing the common causes will hold all of us accountable for breaking the cycle.
Budget Director
Page three
Our fragmented programmatic approach is too expensive.
$14.1Tper year — the economic burden of ACE exposure in the US adult population

This estimate is likely conservative: childhood adversity is only one part of a larger risk cascade. Adversity in the home interacts with experiences in schools, neighborhoods, and communities, creating reinforcing pathways that increase health, social, and economic costs across the life course and often into the next generation.

“Success isn't just about money — it is about how we invest all our resources including people, place, stewardship, knowledge, and funds. Every public dollar we don't need to spend on a crisis is a dollar that can stay in the pocket of a taxpayer who really needs it.
State Legislator

How it works

We don't fix one problem at a time.

The Fund's work rests on a single shift. One way leans on people to compensate, fixing what's broken one piece at a time. The other restores the conditions of the living system, so wellbeing can flow. Drag the handle: it's the same community, blocked or connected.

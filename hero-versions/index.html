The Self-Healing Communities Fund is building a new model that helps communities and public systems to align people, funding, and practices around the common causes of lifelong wellbeing.

Self-Healing Communities Fund

It begins with seeing differently.

We keep treating and financing connected problems as separate ones. Homelessness, behavioral health, chronic disease, addiction, family instability, violence, and problems at school and work are shaped by common causes.

Page one

Risk can build over time.

The Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs) discoveries show how risk can accumulate across childhood, and have cascading effects in health, learning, justice, family stability, work, and involvement with public systems across a lifetime.

Page two

ACE-related risk has outgrown the systems built to manage it.

Adults today carry a substantially higher burden of childhood adversity into parenting years than the generation before them.

27%increase in average ACE score among adults 25–39, 2009–2010 to 2023–2024
1 in 4adults that age now report 4+ ACEs, up from about 1 in 6 a decade earlier

This creates a structural mismatch. The public systems responding to the consequences of adversity were largely built in an earlier, lower-prevalence era — before we understood how ACE-related risk can accumulate across the life course, cross systems, and carry forward to the next generation.

Page three

Our fragmented programmatic approach is too expensive.

$14.1 Trillion/Yearthe economic burden of ACE exposure in the US adult population.

This estimate is likely conservative: childhood adversity is only one part of a larger risk cascade. Adversity in the home interacts with experiences in schools, neighborhoods, and communities, creating reinforcing pathways that increase health, social, and economic costs across the life course and often into the next generation.

Page four

Find where change has the greatest reach.

Popular ideas about what causes risk — and what protects against it — do not always hold up under careful analysis. And not every condition or intervention has equal influence.

SHCF brings rigorous epidemiology together with community knowledge and lived experience to test assumptions, identify the drivers of risk, and find the protective and healing conditions that may have disproportionate — or “keystone” — effects. Communities and public systems can then learn from what happens, refine their understanding, and adapt their portfolios over time.

A different way of working

SHCF brings evidence, community knowledge, learning, and financing together in a continuing cycle.

Pick a step

Five steps wind outward from the core. Choose any one and the path lights from the centre out to it — what it means appears here.

Risk
Stewardship

  1. Find leverage

    See connected risk and healing trajectories across systems, the life course, and generations. Use rigorous evidence and community knowledge to identify where change may have the greatest reach.

  2. Build effective portfolios

    Bring together community knowledge, lived experience, research, practice, public-system authority, and resources to build locally tailored portfolios that change underlying risk trajectories and strengthen protective capacity. Investments work together across the places where risk develops, building on the relationships, assets, and capacities already present.

  3. Learn continuously across investments

    Create shared feedback so communities and partners can learn together whether risk is changing, which benefits cascade across systems, where public costs change, and what should adapt, be sustained, or scale.

  4. Finance differently

    Finance portfolios that reduce risk earlier, strengthen community capacity, support learning, and create benefits across systems. Track the human, economic, and fiscal value created — and align incentives so public systems are rewarded for reducing risk earlier, not only responding to its consequences.

  5. Make improvement durable

    Connect learning, public financing, provider sustainability, and reinvestment so successful change does not disappear when a grant ends. Create mechanisms to reinvest financial gains from improved outcomes into risk reduction and community capacity.

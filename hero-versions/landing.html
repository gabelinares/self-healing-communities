The contrast

Two ways to run a community system.

The Fund's work rests on a single distinction. One kind of system depends on people compensating for poor design. The other builds the intelligence into the environment itself.

Self-organizing · healing-centered

Intelligence embedded in the place.

  • Intelligence is built into the environment
  • Decision-making is distributed
  • People orient and self-navigate
  • Smooth flow, far less intervention
Fragmented · high-friction

Depends on constant human rescue.

  • People compensate for poor design
  • Creates bottlenecks and burnout
  • Reactive, arrives after the harm
  • Scales badly

The Fund finances the move from one to the other, and measures the difference so communities and states can see it.

What the Fund builds

We build the infrastructure, not the individual fix.

Our focus is not any single program or person. It is the financing, the coordination, and the evidence that let communities and states make better decisions about what to build next.

01

Financing & incentives

A funding and management model that rewards the shift from high-friction systems toward self-organizing ones.

02

Measurement & evaluation

Shared infrastructure for innovation and evaluation, so communities and states have better decision-making information.

03

A library of keystone experiences

We catalog the experiences that cascade into many outcomes over time, on the risk side and the healing side.

04

Life-course research

Two epidemiologists study how experience accumulates across a life, and across generations.

The work is the system, not the symptom. Build the conditions, then watch what becomes possible.

The life course

How experience accumulates — over a life, and across generations.

Our epidemiologists study life-course trajectories: how keystone experiences stack up, stage by stage, and how one generation's story shapes the next. Turn the pages.

The movement

Building self-healing communities.

The Self-Healing Communities Fund is a project of ACE Interface, founded by Dr. Robert Anda, a Co-Principal Investigator of the Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACE) Study, and Laura Porter, an expert in applying the ACE Study to community- and state-based system innovation.

For years that work has been a bridge between disciplines, service sectors, and diverse communities, spreading the science of how experience shapes health. The Fund is the next step: the financing and the evidence that turn understanding into infrastructure.

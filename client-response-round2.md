# Draft response to Laura (forwardable to team) — Round 2

Hi Laura,

Thank you, and please thank the team for me. The reactions mean a lot, and these are exactly the kind of comments that make the work better. I'm glad the color, the type, and the interactive feeling are landing, and that it already reads as a step up from the slides.

I want to answer the most important thread first, because several people raised it from different angles: will someone understand what the Self-Healing Communities Fund is early enough? That instinct is right, and it's the most valuable note in the batch. The current concept asks a visitor to travel through the whole story before the Fund comes into focus. We can fix that by stating the big idea plainly near the top, and then letting people choose how deep they want to go from there.

The site you shared, Investing in Flourishing, is a good touchstone for that. It tells you what the effort is in the first screen, then sends you to separate pages for the detail. I'd like to borrow that logic while keeping the warmth and movement your team responded to, so the homepage carries one clear idea and the heavier content lives a click away instead of all in a single scroll.

On photography: the close-up you all loved is now front and center in my plan for the page. It's the kind of real, human image that carries the message better than any stock photo, and I'd love more like it. If the team has access to real community photographs, gatherings, elders and children, everyday local life, those will do more for us than anything I can source.

The data-storytelling examples your colleague sent are beautiful, and they share one thing: each tells a single, focused story really well (the Peru piece especially). That points to where I need your help, because the content is yours to own and I can't write it alone.

A few simple questions would help me aim the next pass. None of these need long answers, and even a rough reply to one or two moves us forward:

1. **The one idea.** If a visitor remembers a single sentence after leaving, what should it be?
2. **Time on the page.** Do you picture someone spending about a minute getting the gist, or settling in for several minutes to read and explore? This decides how much we put on the first screen.
3. **One story or many.** Is this telling one central story, or becoming a home for many community stories over time? Both are great, they're just built differently.
4. **Who matters most.** Of funders, policy partners, and the general public, who do you most want this first page to win over? We can serve all three, but the order shapes the design.
5. **The next step.** After someone reads it, what do you hope they do? Sign up for updates, get in touch, share it, or simply come away understanding the idea?
6. **The words.** For the short headlines and the story content, can I count on the team for drafts, or would it help if I propose placeholder copy for you to react to?

No rush on all of these. I'll hold the next design pass until I hear your direction, so we build toward the right thing rather than reworking it later.

Thanks again. This is a strong batch of feedback and I'm genuinely excited about where it's heading.

Gabe

# ACE Interface — Project Briefing

## Background
ACE Interface is a company founded by Dr. Robert Anda and Laura Porter, co-originators of the Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACE) Study. Their work focuses on building self-healing communities by helping states and organizations apply ACE science to real community systems.

The ACE Interface site (aceinterface.com) exists but is dated and serves a redesign focusing on their core goal and messaging.

## Objective
Redesign and build a new website ACE Interface

Create a landing page following the same look and feel for the Self-Healing Communities Fund that:

- Communicates the Fund's mission clearly to its primary audiences
- Uses visual storytelling and interactive design to explain the core concept - not just words on a page
- Follows the existing ACE Interface color palette and fonts

This project is meant to set the visual direction that Phase 2 (the brand video) will follow.

For the Self-healing Communities Fund, the client has a strong conceptual anchor for this project, and it should inform both the content and the design logic of the site itself.

## The contrast
The Fund's work is built on a distinction between two types of community systems:

**Fragmented, high-friction system:**
- Depends on constant human intervention
- Creates bottlenecks and burnout
- Reactive, not designed for flow
- Scales badly

**Self-organizing, healing-centered system:**
- Intelligence embedded in the environment
- People orient and self-navigate
- Distributed coordination, less friction
- Reduces demand for costly public services

## How this shapes the website
The wayfinding principle should not just be a story on the page - it should be felt in how the site works. Visitors should be able to orient themselves quickly, navigate intuitively, and understand the Fund's mission without needing someone to walk them through it.

The site itself should demonstrate what the Fund is trying to build.

## Visual storytelling
The client wants to communicate this contrast visually, not through text alone. Potential directions to explore:

- Split-screen animations contrasting the two system types
- Line-drawing motion graphics showing crowd flow or community movement
- An interactive element where visitors can explore the community ecosystem
- A scroll-based narrative that moves from friction to flow

The setting the client has proposed: a multi-generational community campus or riverfront festival with libraries, childcare, gardens, recreation, health resources, gathering spaces, and market areas. Not a service map, but a living community ecosystem.

## Target Audience
To be confirmed with client, but likely a mix of:

- Public health and government funders
- State and community-level policy partners
- Organizations working in community health, resilience, and ACE prevention
- General public/community advocates

## Design Direction Notes
Based on client conversations, keep in mind:

- Avoid making the site feel futuristic, over-polished, or technologically controlled
- It should feel human, warm, local, and achievable
- The emotional message: communities can work well when designed for it
- Multi-generational and diverse representation in any imagery or illustration
- The contrast between the two system types should be emotionally felt, not just explained
